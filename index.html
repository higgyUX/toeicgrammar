Verb tenses

A tense tells us when something happens. On the TOEIC, the time words in the sentence tell you which tense to choose.

The eight tenses you need most

TenseUse it forExampleTime clues
Present simplehabits, factsShe works in Tokyo.every day, usually, always
Present continuoushappening nowShe is working now.now, at the moment, currently
Present perfectstarted in the past and connected to nowShe has worked here for three years.since, for, already, yet, so far
Past simplea finished actionShe worked late yesterday.yesterday, last week, in 2020, two days ago
Past continuousin progress at one moment in the pastShe was working at 9 p.m.at 9 p.m. yesterday, while
Past perfecthappened before another past actionShe had left before I arrived.before, by the time
Future simpleplans, decisions, predictionsShe will work tomorrow.tomorrow, next week
Future perfectfinished before a future timeShe will have finished by 5 p.m.by + a future time

Four rules that win points

since + starting point, for + length of time → present perfect

I have lived here since 2019.   He has studied English for two years.

finished time word → past simple

Words like yesterday, last week, in 2019, two days ago point to the past simple. We saw him yesterday. The office opened in 2019.

earlier past action → past perfect (had + past participle)

When I arrived, the meeting had started. (First the meeting started, then I arrived.)

when / before / after / as soon as → present simple, even for the future

I will call you when I arrive. (Not: when I will arrive.)

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: She works here since 2015. ✓Right: She has worked here since 2015. "Since 2015" starts in the past and continues now. That needs present perfect.
  • ✗Wrong: I have seen him yesterday. ✓Right: I saw him yesterday. "Yesterday" is a finished time. Present perfect cannot be used with a finished time.
  • ✗Wrong: He will call you when he will arrive. ✓Right: He will call you when he arrives. After "when", we use present simple even when the meaning is future.

Subject and verb agreement

A singular subject needs a singular verb. A plural subject needs a plural verb. This is one of the most tested points in TOEIC Part 5.

Find the real subject. Ignore the words between the subject and the verb.

The list of new products is ready. (The subject is "list", not "products".)

Takes a singular verb

  • Uncountable nouns: information, money, advice, news, furniture. The news is good.
  • Everyone, someone, nobody, each, every, either, neither. Each student has a book.
  • A gerund (V-ing) as the subject. Reading is fun.
  • An amount of money, time or distance. Ten dollars is enough.
  • Subjects ending in -s that are one thing: physics, measles. Physics is hard.
  • A pair of + plural noun. A pair of shoes is on sale.

Takes a plural verb

  • Plural nouns. The books are here.
  • Both, many, several, few. Both managers are late.
  • Two subjects joined by and. Tom and Anna are ready.
  • Pair nouns: glasses, pants, scissors. My glasses are broken.
  • The + adjective for a group: the rich, the poor. The poor need help.

Tricky patterns

PatternRuleExample
Either… or / Neither… norThe verb agrees with the second noun.Neither the boss nor the workers are here.
A, along with B / as well asThe verb agrees with the first noun.The manager, along with his staff, is here.
A number of + plural nounPlural verb. It means "many".A number of people are waiting.
The number of + plural nounSingular verb. It means one number.The number of people is small.
Most of / some of / all of / half of + nounThe verb agrees with the noun after "of".Most of the water is gone. Most of the students are here.
There is / There areThe verb agrees with the noun after it.There is a problem. There are two problems.
Team, family, staffSingular when the group acts as one unit. Plural only when members act as individuals (less common).The team is ready.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: The list of items are long. ✓Right: The list of items is long. The subject is "list" (singular). "Items" is only part of the phrase "of items".
  • ✗Wrong: Neither the boss nor the workers is here. ✓Right: Neither the boss nor the workers are here. With neither… nor, the verb matches the closest noun: "workers" (plural).
  • ✗Wrong: The number of applicants are growing. ✓Right: The number of applicants is growing. "The number of" is one number, so the verb is singular.

Gerunds and infinitives

A gerund is a verb with -ing (working). An infinitive is to + verb (to work). Each verb in English chooses one pattern, and you must learn which.

Verb + V-ing

enjoy, avoid, consider, suggest, mind, keep, finish

  • She avoided answering the email.
  • We finished writing the report.

Verb + to + verb

decide, hope, plan, want, offer, agree, need, expect

  • He decided to leave early.
  • They plan to open a new shop.

Verb + person + to + verb

encourage, allow, ask, tell, remind, invite

  • The boss allowed us to leave early.
  • She reminded him to call.

Preposition + V-ing

for, in, at, about, of, by + V-ing

  • Thank you for helping me.
  • She is good at solving problems.

Verbs that change meaning

Verb+ V-ing (usually the past)+ to + verb (usually the future)
rememberI remember locking the door. (I recall doing it.)Remember to lock the door. (Do not forget.)
forgetI forgot meeting her before. (I did not recall the past event.)He forgot to send the email. (He did not do it.)
stopShe stopped working there. (She quit.)She stopped to answer the phone. (She paused in order to answer.)
tryTry restarting your laptop. (Do it as an experiment.)Try to arrive early. (Make an effort.)
regretI regret saying that. (I am sorry I said it.)We regret to inform you… (formal bad news)

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: She avoided to answer the email. ✓Right: She avoided answering the email. "Avoid" is always followed by V-ing.
  • ✗Wrong: He decided leaving early. ✓Right: He decided to leave early. "Decide" is followed by to + verb.
  • ✗Wrong: The manager allowed us leaving early. ✓Right: The manager allowed us to leave early. "Allow + person" is followed by to + verb.
4

Modal verbs

Modal verbs add a feeling to the sentence: ability, advice, duty, possibility or permission. TOEIC questions often test how strong the feeling is.

modal + base verb

No to, no -s, no -ing after a modal. She can swim. He should apply. They must leave.

ModalUse it forExample
can / couldability, possibilityShe can solve the problem easily.
may / mightpossibility. May can also mean permission.It might rain later. You may leave early.
must / have tostrong obligation (a rule)You must submit the report by 5 p.m.
should / ought toadviceYou should check your email.
will / wouldfuture; polite requestI will join the call. Would you help me?

might (weakest) → could → should → must (strongest)

Choose the modal that matches how strong the sentence is. A rule needs must. A suggestion needs should.

must not ≠ don't have to

You must not park here. (It is forbidden.)   You don't have to park here. (It is not necessary. You can choose.)

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: She can speaks three languages. ✓Right: She can speak three languages. A modal verb is followed by the base verb. Never add -s after a modal.
  • ✗Wrong: You must to wear a helmet. ✓Right: You must wear a helmet. Do not put "to" between a modal and the verb.
  • ✗Wrong: He should going to the meeting. ✓Right: He should go to the meeting. Do not add -ing after a modal. Use the base verb.

If sentences (conditionals)

An if sentence has two parts: a condition (the if-part) and a result. The tense of each part tells you if the situation is real or imaginary.

TypeFormMeaningExample
ZeroIf + present simple, present simplea general truthIf water reaches 100°C, it boils.
Type 1If + present simple, will + base verbreal future possibilityIf it rains, we will cancel the trip.
Type 2If + past simple, would + base verbimaginary nowIf I were you, I would apply today.
Type 3If + past perfect, would have + past participleimaginary past (too late)If he had studied, he would have passed.
MixedIf + past perfect, would + base verbpast cause, present resultIf she had left earlier, she would be here now.

Tips

  • Never put will directly after if. Say "If it rains", not "If it will rain".
  • In Type 2, use were for every subject in test English: If I were, if he were.
  • The result part uses would have + past participle for Type 3. The if-part uses had + past participle.
  • The if-part can come second. Then you do not need a comma: We will cancel the trip if it rains.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: If it will rain, we will cancel the trip. ✓Right: If it rains, we will cancel the trip. No "will" in the if-part. Use present simple.
  • ✗Wrong: If I would have time, I would help you. ✓Right: If I had time, I would help you. "Would" belongs in the result part. The if-part uses past simple.
  • ✗Wrong: If she had called, I would helped her. ✓Right: If she had called, I would have helped her. Type 3 needs "would have" + past participle in the result part.

Passive voice

Use the passive when the thing that receives the action is more important than the person who does it. It is very common in formal business English.

be + past participle (V3)

Active: The company sent the invoice.   Passive: The invoice was sent (by the company).

The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

TensePassive formExample
Present simpleam / is / are + V3Emails are sent daily.
Past simplewas / were + V3The document was updated yesterday.
Present perfecthas / have been + V3The files have been uploaded.
Future simplewill be + V3The package will be delivered tomorrow.
Modalmodal + be + V3Tickets must be purchased in advance.

How to know when to use the passive

  • The subject cannot do the action. A package cannot deliver itself, so: The package will be delivered.
  • You see by + the doer: The report was written by Tom.
  • There is no object after the verb: The room was cleaned.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: The files are send every day. ✓Right: The files are sent every day. After "be", use the past participle (sent), not the base verb.
  • ✗Wrong: The report was write by Tom. ✓Right: The report was written by Tom. Irregular verbs need the correct participle: write, wrote, written.
  • ✗Wrong: The package will deliver tomorrow. ✓Right: The package will be delivered tomorrow. The package receives the action, so the verb must be passive.

Word forms

Ending clues

Part of speechCommon endingsExamples
Noun-tion, -ment, -ness, -ityinformation, payment, kindness, ability

Be careful: a few words end in -ly but are adjectives, such as friendly and early.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: She spoke clear to the group. ✓Right: She spoke clearly to the group. To describe a verb (spoke), use an adverb.
  • ✗Wrong: They made an important decide. ✓Right: They made an important decision. After "an important" we need a noun, not a verb.
  • ✗Wrong: The process is complication. ✓Right: The process is complicated. After "is", we describe the subject with an adjective.
